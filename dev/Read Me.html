Liner notes

How Glass Crate came to be

I wanted a way to download video and audio to use in mashups and remixes. I looked at the video and audio downloaders out there, and most were loaded with spyware. Some had malware built right in.

Rather than take that risk, I learned that everything could be done from the Mac's Terminal with Homebrew and a couple of free, open-source tools. Glass Crate started as a small AppleScript, a simple window for running those Homebrew commands. After some development, it grew into a full Mac application that lives and breathes on the Mac, and Homebrew wasn't needed anymore. Now the app runs safely on its own, with nothing extra installed beyond the few tools it actually uses.

From the start, the goal was transparency. Glass Crate tells you exactly what it uses, how it uses it, and where it's kept. Its uninstaller removes not just the app, but every tool it added, leaving only the videos and music you downloaded.

I wanted a tool I could trust. That's how this app came to be.

That's why it's called Glass Crate: like a crate made of glass, there's nothing hidden inside. It's also open source, so anyone can read every line of the code on GitHub.

Since starting this project, there have been plenty of ideas, and they're collected in the roadmap below. Version 1.3 brings a new red look. Version 1.2 added a browser right inside the app, so you can find a video or track on YouTube or SoundCloud and add it to your crate with one click, plus themes and an Interface Size setting for any screen. It's still a simple tool that does exactly what it's supposed to, and does it well.

Features

What it does

Browse right inside the app

A phone-sized browser on the left side of the window. Pick YouTube or SoundCloud, find a video or track, and click Add to Crate. No copying and pasting. The website sits inside its own outlined "screen," so it's always clear what's the site and what's Glass Crate.

A Download Queue

Paste a link (or use the browser), click Add to Crate, and keep going. Downloads line up in a queue that slides out beside the window and run one after another, with the one in progress at the top and what's up next listed below it.

Video and audio, all at once

Check any combination of MP4 video, MP3, WAV, and AIFF. Each is saved as its own file, so you can grab the video, an MP3, and a WAV in one go.

The quality you want

Video downloads at the best quality available, or choose 1080p, 720p, 480p, or 360p in Settings. If a video doesn't have your choice, you get the closest quality below it. MP3s use the highest MP3 quality. SoundCloud tracks are saved as audio only.

Uncompressed for production

WAV and AIFF are ready for your music software. AIFF is the Apple-friendly version of WAV.

Album art and tags

MP3s get the video's thumbnail as square album art. Every file is tagged with the title, the artist (the official artist when one is listed, otherwise the channel), and the video title as the album. Audio files are also marked "Encoded by: Glass Crate," the way most apps that make audio files label their work.

Always know what's happening

Each download shows its thumbnail, title, and a progress bar, with size and speed, like "Video (file 1 of 2): downloading picture 42% · 41.2 MB of 98.6 MB · 2.3 MB/s." If something fails, you see what went wrong and a Try Again button.

Pick up where you left off

Quit with downloads running and Glass Crate asks first, then saves the queue. Next time, Resume continues each one from where it stopped, and finished formats aren't downloaded again.

Keep it on top

The pin keeps Glass Crate and its queue above your other windows, so you can keep browsing and still see your downloads.

Files go where you want

Videos and audio each have their own folder, and WAV/AIFF can have a third. Or turn on "Ask where to save each time." When a download finishes, a button opens its folder.

Updates, the Mac way

Sites change often, so the tools get updates. Settings → Updates lists each one, and you choose what to download and install. A small "Update available" at the bottom of the window lets you know. No pop-ups, and never in the middle of a download.

Your look, your size

Four Glass Crate themes (Light, Crimson, Dark, and Blackout, in the red of the app icon) plus the Mac's own Light, Dark, and Match System. Interface Size, from 70% to 130%, makes everything fit any screen, from a 13-inch laptop to a big monitor.

Transparency

Exactly what gets installed

Glass Crate itself is small. To do its work, it relies on free, open-source tools. The first time you open it, a setup screen lists each one with links to its official site, and nothing is downloaded until you click Begin Setup.

FileWhat it doesComes from
yt-dlp Downloads the video and audio from the link you paste. yt-dlp on GitHub
FFmpeg Joins video and audio, converts to MP3, WAV, or AIFF, and adds album art. ffmpeg.org, Mac build by Martin Riedl
FFprobe FFmpeg's companion. Checks each file before it's converted. Same as FFmpeg
Deno Helps yt-dlp read YouTube's pages so downloads keep working. deno.com
4 files · about 200 MB Kept in one folder: ~/Library/Application Support/Glass Crate
  • No Homebrew
  • No password needed
  • No Terminal
  • No account or sign-in
  • No ads, analytics, or tracking from Glass Crate (sites you browse show their own ads)
  • Nothing running in the background
  • Nothing installed outside its own folder

Glass Crate only goes online to download the links you paste, to look up a video's or track's title and picture when you add it to the queue (from YouTube or SoundCloud), to fetch these four tools from the sites listed above, and to check for updates to them.

Getting started

Installing

  1. Open Glass Crate {{VERSION}}.dmg and drag Glass Crate onto the Applications folder next to it. Then eject the disk.
  2. Open Glass Crate from Applications. The first time, macOS will stop it, because the app isn't registered with Apple. Close that message, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.
  3. On the setup screen, read through the tools and click Begin Setup. A progress bar shows each of the 4 files downloading, with its size and speed. This usually takes a few minutes.

If you change your mind, click Quit on the setup screen. If Glass Crate closes before setup finishes, it deletes anything it downloaded, so nothing is left behind.

Everyday use

Downloading

  1. Check what you want on the right: Video: MP4, and any of Audio: MP3, WAV, AIFF.
  2. In the browser on the left, pick YouTube or SoundCloud and find a video or track. Or copy a link from anywhere and paste it into the box on the right.
  3. Click Add to Crate (or press Return in the link box). The Download Queue slides out and the download starts.
  4. When a download says Done!, click Open Video Folder or Open Audio Folder to find your files.
Folders and settings

Where your files go

By default

  • Videos go to Movies/Glass Crate
  • Audio goes to Music/Glass Crate, including WAV and AIFF
  • Each folder is created the first time something is saved to it

Changing it

Click the gear at the top right, or press ⌘,, to open Settings. Under Downloads you can:

  • Pick a different Video or Audio folder
  • Give WAV and AIFF their own folder, like the one your music software uses
  • Choose the video quality (Best available unless you change it)
  • Turn on "Ask where to save each time"

Themes and Interface Size are under General.

Leaving no trace

Uninstalling

Open Settings → Uninstall, or choose Uninstall Glass Crate… from the Glass Crate menu. The Uninstall tab lists exactly what goes and what stays. After you confirm, it removes:

Removed

  • The app (moved to the Trash)
  • All 4 tools and their folder
  • Your settings: folders, appearance, and checkbox choices
  • The saved Download Queue, and any tool updates that were downloaded but not installed
  • Partial files from unfinished downloads (only the ones Glass Crate itself was writing)
  • The browser's history, cookies, and sign-ins

Kept

Every video and audio file you downloaded stays exactly where it is.

Use Uninstall rather than dragging the app to the Trash. Dragging it removes only the app and leaves the 200 MB of tools behind.

Good to know

Questions and fixes

A download failed. What do I do?

Click Try Again. Sites sometimes refuse a single request, and a second try usually works. If it keeps failing, open Settings → Updates and click Check Now. An update fixes most problems, since sites change often.

Does it work with sites other than YouTube?

YouTube is the only officially supported site. Links from some other sites may also work, since yt-dlp understands many of them, but they aren't tested or supported. If a link has no video, like a music track, uncheck MP4 and keep an audio format checked.

It says YouTube asked Glass Crate to slow down. What does that mean?

YouTube limits how many requests it accepts from one internet connection. Glass Crate already goes easy, one download at a time with a short pause between them, but if YouTube still says slow down, the queue waits a few minutes with a countdown and then carries on by itself. Click Try Now to skip the wait. If it keeps happening, give it a few hours. A VPN can make it happen more often.

Will WAV or AIFF sound better than the original?

No. YouTube's audio is already compressed, so an uncompressed file can't add quality that isn't there. What it does guarantee is no further loss, and a format your music software handles easily.

Why does macOS warn me the first time I open it?

Apps that aren't registered with Apple's paid developer program show this warning once. The Open Anyway button in Privacy & Security lets it run. Removing this step is on the roadmap.

Where can I read more inside the app?

Choose About Glass Crate from the Glass Crate menu at the top left. To see what changed in each version, click the version number at the bottom of the window.

What's next

Roadmap

Ideas collected along the way, saved for after this release. Plans can change.

Next

  • The Crate: one place to see, play, and clean out everything you've downloaded, and delete the tracks you don't want, one at a time.
  • Skip one format mid-download and move on to the rest.
  • Retry automatically when a site refuses a single request.

  • Make your own theme, with your own colors.

Updates and other sites

  • Updates for Glass Crate itself, not just its tools, from madebyjp.net.
  • Official support beyond YouTube, starting with SoundCloud.
  • Optional sign-in for sites that require it, off by default and clearly explained.

Bigger ideas

  • File conversion: drop in a WAV and get an MP3, pull the audio out of a video, and convert whole batches, with album art from an image you pick.
  • Channel Vault, a separate backup tool for creators: enter your channel, see your videos as thumbnails, and back up the ones you choose, with their titles, descriptions, and upload dates.
  • Apple notarization, which removes the one-time "Open Anyway" step.
Use it well

A note on responsible use

Only download content you own or have permission to save. Glass Crate is built for working with your own material and material you have the rights to use.
